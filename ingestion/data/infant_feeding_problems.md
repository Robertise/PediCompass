# Infant Feeding Problems

## Symptoms
- Baby is taking less than half their normal amount of feed in 24 hours.
- Prolonged feeding times (taking more than 30 minutes to finish a bottle).
- Baby falls asleep consistently before finishing the feed.
- Unsettled or unsatisfied even after long, frequent breast or bottle feeds.
- Fewer wet or dirty nappies than expected (e.g., fewer than 6 heavy wet nappies in 24 hours after day 5).

## Warning Signs (Red Flags)
- **Dysphagia (Swallowing Difficulties):** Coughing, choking, or sputtering during feeds.
- **Breathing Changes:** Wheezing or "wet" breathing sounds during feeding.
- **Color Changes:** Turning pale, red, or blue around the lips or face during or after feeds.
- **Lethargy:** Extreme reluctance to feed or severe lethargy.
- **Distress:** Severe distress or anxiety during mealtimes.
- **Sudden Changes:** Abrupt changes in feeding habits, especially if accompanied by other signs of illness (e.g., fever).

## Care Actions
- **Positioning:** Ensure proper positioning and latch (deep latch for breastfeeding). 
- **Environment:** Feed when the baby shows early hunger cues (rooting, wriggling) and is calm.
- **Safety Precaution:** Never add extra powder to formula or dilute it with extra water. Avoid thickening feeds without professional advice.
- **Professional Assessment:** Contact a health visitor, midwife, or pediatrician for issues like persistent pain during nursing, poor weight gain, or suspected tongue-tie.
- **Immediate Action:** Seek urgent medical care for any "Red Flag" signs like choking, color changes, or severe lethargy.
