# Fever in Children Under 5 Years

## Symptoms
- Elevated body temperature (height of temperature alone does not indicate illness severity).
- May appear unwell, fussy, or distressed.

## Warning Signs (Red Flags)
- **Skin/Appearance:** Pale, mottled, ashen, or blue skin, lips, or tongue. Non-blanching rash (does not disappear under pressure).
- **Responsiveness:** No response to social cues, decreased level of consciousness, difficulty waking up, or extremely lethargic.
- **Breathing:** Grunting, tachypnoea (fast breathing), chest indrawing, or nasal flaring.
- **Neurological:** Neck stiffness, bulging fontanelle (in infants), focal neurological signs, or seizures/fits.
- **Other Severe Signs:** Temperature ≥38°C in infants under 3 months. Signs of severe dehydration (prolonged capillary refill time, dry mucous membranes, reduced urine). Fever lasting 5 days or longer. Swelling of a limb or joint; refusal to use a limb.

## Care Actions
- **Measurement:** Measure temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, and capillary refill time. 
- **Comfort:** Use antipyretics (paracetamol or ibuprofen) only if the child is distressed. Do not use both simultaneously unless advised. Note: Resolution of fever after medication does not mean the child is well.
- **Hydration:** Offer regular fluids. Monitor for signs of dehydration (urine output, tears, mouth moisture).
- **Immediate Action:** Seek emergency medical assessment immediately for any "Red Flag" warning signs.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor continuously. Seek face-to-face assessment for intermediate signs (fever for 5+ days, poor feeding, abnormal responsiveness).
