# Childhood Rashes

## Symptoms
- Redness, spots, or generalized rash on the skin.
- May be accompanied by mild itching or localized irritation.
- Most are harmless and result from common viral illnesses, heat, or minor skin irritations.

## Warning Signs (Red Flags)
- **Non-Blanching Rash:** Small purple or red pinpoint spots that do not fade (blanch) when pressed (e.g., with a clear glass). This is a medical emergency.
- **Breathing Issues:** Rash accompanied by wheezing, shortness of breath, or working hard to breathe.
- **Swelling:** Swelling of the face, lips, or tongue.
- **Severe Illness Signs:** The child is unusually lethargic, inconsolable, confused, has a stiff neck, or "doesn't seem right."
- **Infection Signs:** Increased redness, warmth, significant swelling, pus, crusting, or red streaks extending from the rash.
- **High Fever:** Rash with a temperature over 38.9°C (102°F), especially if the child appears ill.

## Care Actions
- **The Glass Test:** If a rash appears, press the side of a clear glass firmly against the skin. If the spots remain visible, seek urgent emergency care.
- **Observation:** Monitor the child's overall behavior. A child who is alert, hydrated, and playful is less likely to have a severe underlying condition.
- **Home Management:** For mild, known rashes (like heat rash), keep the skin clean and dry. Avoid irritants. Use moisturizers for dry skin/eczema as directed.
- **Professional Assessment:** Contact a pediatrician if the rash lasts longer than a few days, does not improve, spreads rapidly, causes significant pain, or if signs of localized infection develop.
