# Respiratory Distress in Children Under 5

## Symptoms
- Increased breathing rate or breathing faster than normal (tachypnoea).
- Noisy breathing, such as mild wheezing (whistling sound) or stridor (harsh sound when inhaling).
- Noticeable effort required to breathe.

## Warning Signs (Red Flags)
- **Breathing Cessation:** Stops breathing or has prolonged pauses in breathing.
- **Color Changes:** Bluish, grayish, or purple tint around the mouth, lips, face, or fingernails. Pale skin.
- **Retractions:** Skin pulling in with each breath (visible between ribs, below the ribcage, or at the base of the neck).
- **Nasal Flaring and Grunting:** Nostrils widening significantly with every breath, or a grunting sound heard during exhalation.
- **Body Positioning:** Head bobbing with each breath, or using neck muscles to breathe.
- **Extreme Lethargy:** Difficulty waking up, unusually tired, restless, or fussy.
- **Severe Impairment:** Inability to speak, cry normally, or feed/suck due to breathing difficulty.

## Care Actions
- **Immediate Action:** Call emergency services or go to the nearest emergency room immediately for any "Red Flag" warning signs (pauses in breathing, color changes, severe retractions, lethargy).
- **Monitoring:** For mild symptoms (e.g., a simple cold), monitor closely, ensure rest, and maintain hydration.
- **Supportive Care:** Use a humidifier or saline drops/nasal suction for nasal congestion as appropriate.
- **Professional Assessment:** Contact a pediatrician if the child has a fever (especially if under 3 months), refuses to eat/drink, or if breathing concerns persist or worsen.
